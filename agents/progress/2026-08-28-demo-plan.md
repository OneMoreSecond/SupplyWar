# 2. Constraint and Assumption

## Constraints

- Keep map data separate from the engine and preserve independent simulation and rendering ticks. Source: [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md).
- Keep the change staged and avoid procedural generation, behavior trees, an event bus, or a general ability framework until one concrete implementation proves the need. Source: simplicity rules in [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md); design judgment.
- Preserve deterministic simulation so AI choices, siege, abilities, and balance scenarios can be replayed in tests. Source: current fixed-step design in [`src/game.ts`](../../src/game.ts); design judgment.
- Use the same legal transport and ability commands for AI and player; AI must not mutate node force or ownership directly. Source: fairness and testability design judgment.
- Keep road crossings valid. Urban density may use crossings where readable. Source: [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md).
- Treat map-schema input as external data: validate invalid versions and fields with explicit errors. Source: coding rules in [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md); current validator in [`src/game.ts`](../../src/game.ts).
- Update maintained formal documentation when behavior changes; retain this progress record and its history as the decision trail. Source: [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md); [`doc/mvp/README.md`](../../doc/mvp/README.md).

# 4. Decisions

## Gameplay decisions

- Use rooted support reachability for version-2 siege. Supply roots never receive siege decay, but normal attacking packets can still capture them. Source: [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); design judgment distinguishing immunity from invulnerability.
- Revise Tutorial 4 to siege an ordinary fortress before taking a non-siegeable enemy base; rebalance the MVP final base for packet capture after the frontline supply cut. Source: compatibility challenge above; design judgment.
- Add one Demo map with 30–40 nodes, approximately 45–60 roads, and 6–8 meaningful resource nodes. These are initial authoring bounds, not engine limits. Source: “dozens” and sparse-resource requirements in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); design judgment.
- Structure the map as player rear, rural flank, dense central urban/resource belt, enemy rear, and one long poor-road ambush route. Source: micro-component requirements in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); design judgment.
- Use Interdiction as the only new destabilizer in the first slice. It reinforces the supply-war identity and can create a temporary unsupported window that becomes permanent if the player captures the exposed supply root. Source: [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); design judgment.
- Defer random events until deterministic play evidence shows that player-triggered disruption is insufficient. Source: uncertainty and balance design judgment.

## Technical decisions

- Introduce `MapConfigV1`, `MapConfigV2`, and one version-aware parse/normalize boundary. Version 2 requires its new rule settings and `Road.travelTimeMultiplier`; the engine consumes one normalized runtime type. Source: current schema boundary in [`src/game.ts`](../../src/game.ts); design judgment.
- Put the AI policy in `src/ai.ts` as a pure decision function that returns at most one command per decision interval. Apply that command through `Simulation` methods. Source: modularity and deterministic-test design judgment.
- Use a small command union: start transport, cancel transport, cast Interdiction, or wait. Do not add a general command bus. Source: simplicity design judgment.
- Score AI actions in this order: save a threatened supplied holding, capture an adjacent resource, attack an adjacent weak/unsupported hostile node, expand into a neutral node, then consolidate. Use node/road IDs as deterministic tie-breakers. Source: AI goals in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); design judgment.
- Define “bad-positioned” for the first AI as an adjacent hostile node that is unrooted, has low force relative to the source, or is a resource. Do not add graph-centrality analysis initially. Source: AI requirement in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); simplicity design judgment.
- Put visibility calculation in `src/visibility.ts` as a pure projection. Keep discovered-node memory in page state because it affects presentation, not simulation outcomes. Source: simulation/render separation in [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md); design judgment.
- When implementing fog and shapes, move cohesive Canvas drawing and hit testing from `src/main.ts` into one view module; retain timing, page flow, AI orchestration, and command handling in `src/main.ts`. Source: current responsibilities in [`src/main.ts`](../../src/main.ts); narrow/deep-interface design judgment.
- Extend the existing editor for version-2 fields after rules stabilize; do not build a separate Demo editor. Source: existing editor contract in [`src/editor.ts`](../../src/editor.ts); project map-data constraint in [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md).

## Player-facing decisions

- Use star bases, square resources, and circular ordinary nodes. Keep ownership color independent from shape. Source: [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); design judgment.
- Map road width to stroke thickness and poor-road latency to a repeated broken/sleeper pattern; do not encode both properties with thickness. Source: road requirements in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); visual-clarity judgment.
- Show Interdiction as a named HUD action with cooldown, target preview, a visible disrupted-road timer, and explicit invalid-target guidance. Source: mechanism-presentation request in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); design judgment.
- Show visible nodes normally, discovered nodes as neutral low-contrast silhouettes without force/owner, and undiscovered nodes not at all. Source: fog requirement in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); design judgment.
- Bundle Barlow locally and keep a system sans-serif fallback so the GitHub Pages build has no runtime font dependency. Source: typography requirement in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); [Google Fonts Barlow description](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/barlow/DESCRIPTION.en_us.html); deployment constraint in [`vite.config.ts`](../../vite.config.ts).

## Delivery phases and gates

### Phase A — Freeze the rules in tests

1. Add failing tests for version-1 compatibility, strict version-2 validation, explicit upgrade output, and `travelTimeMultiplier` latency.
2. Add failing siege tests for root immunity, a rooted multi-hop support chain, an unrooted cycle, a rooted cycle, source capture, and direction change after capture.
3. Implement the version-aware parser/runtime config and rooted-supply calculation with no UI changes.
4. Migrate maintained maps to version 2, revise Tutorial 4 and the MVP final-base balance, and keep their scripted solutions passing.

Phase A source: schema and siege requirements in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md), current version boundary in [`src/game.ts`](../../src/game.ts), and TDD guidance in [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md).

Gate A: all existing mechanics and level flows pass under the maintained version-2 maps; imported version-1 maps retain legacy behavior. Source: regression design judgment.

### Phase B — Build a challenging opponent

1. Add an immutable AI observation and start/cancel/wait results; extend the union with Interdiction in Phase D.
2. Implement one decision every configurable number of simulation seconds, with reserve and route-cancellation rules.
3. Test defense, resource priority, weak-target attack, neutral expansion, cancellation, deterministic tie-breaking, and no-command cases.
4. Run AI through the same `Simulation` commands used by the player.

Phase B source: AI behavior requested in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md) and deterministic modularity design judgment.

Gate B: on small fixtures, the AI expands, protects at least one threatened holding, targets a resource before a stronger ordinary node, and produces identical actions for identical states. Source: AI requirement and deterministic-test judgment.

### Phase C — Author and test the large-map baseline

1. Add the `demo` catalog kind and `maps/demo.json` after the MVP final exam.
2. Author the regional topology and explicit production/width/latency variation without fog or Interdiction enabled initially.
3. Add scripted scenario checks for reachable bases, sparse production, urban/rural density contrast, meaningful alternate routes, and AI progression.
4. Play the fully visible baseline and tune only map values and AI weights.

Phase C source: large-map stages and micro-components requested in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md), plus staged-validation design judgment.

Gate C: representative runs show expansion, first resource contact, a measurable advantage change, and eventual victory; no run remains unchanged for more than three minutes after first contact. Source: match stages in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); validation judgment.

If Gate C fails, fix topology, economy, route lifecycle, or AI before adding fog or abilities. Source: staged-diagnosis judgment.

### Phase D — Add information and destabilization

1. Add and test visible/discovered/unknown projection for nodes, roads, transports, labels, hit testing, and status text.
2. Add the player Interdiction action and the same AI command with configured duration/cooldown.
3. Prove that disruption pauses dispatch, preserves in-flight packets, breaks rooted support during its timer, recovers exactly once, and cannot target hidden or allied routes.
4. Give the AI a conservative Interdiction rule: target a visible hostile support route to a contested resource/frontline, with deterministic tie-breaking.

Phase D source: fog and destabilization requirements in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md), plus the gameplay decisions above.

Gate D: in browser play, the player can explain why a node became unsupported, distinguish discovery from live intelligence, and use disruption to create a capturable window. Source: mechanism clarity judgment.

### Phase E — Present the game clearly

1. Extract the cohesive game-view code when the new rendering work starts.
2. Add base/resource/ordinary shapes, width-aware roads, poor-road styling, fog states, disruption feedback, an ability HUD, and defeat handling.
3. Bundle Barlow Regular/SemiBold and its license; preserve readable fallbacks and keyboard focus states.
4. Add one concise Demo briefing and tactical hint explaining that resources anchor supply and Interdiction creates a temporary break.
5. Verify zoomed-out readability and interaction on the production build.

Phase E source: shape, font, and mechanism-presentation requirements in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md), plus the player-facing decisions above.

Gate E: a player can identify node kind, ownership, live force, road capacity, poor-road latency, support direction, siege, fog, and disruption without relying on color alone. Source: UI requirements in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); accessibility design judgment.

### Phase F — Balance and decide whether it is fun

1. Record at least five completed human runs, using at least two players if available; explicitly record the limitation if only one tester is available.
2. Record real duration, first contact time, ownership changes, front reversals, Interdiction uses, decisive supply cuts, winner, and short player comments.
3. Tune map JSON, AI weights, and configured ability values; do not change core formulas merely to hit duration.
4. Add production-browser coverage and inspect screenshots for early expansion, contested front, fog discovery, active disruption, decisive advantage, victory, and defeat.
5. Update the maintained gameplay, map, technology, UX, and validation documents only after the implemented behavior is verified.

Phase F source: “really fun” and 10–15 minute goals in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md), current validation practice in [`doc/mvp/validation.md`](../../doc/mvp/validation.md), and measurement design judgment.

Gate F acceptance:

- Median completed human match duration is 10–15 minutes. Source: [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); measurement design judgment.
- At least four of five runs reach all four intended stages: expansion, resource-front contact, advantage break, and finish. Source: [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); measurement design judgment.
- At least three of five runs contain a front reversal or a successful temporary disruption that becomes a permanent territorial advantage. Source: destabilization goal in [`doc/Demo.md`](../../doc/Demo.md); measurement design judgment.
- Testers can state what caused the decisive change; unexplained fog losses or ability effects are failures even if the match ends on time. Source: clarity and fun design judgment.
- Typecheck, unit/scenario tests, production build, diff check, browser workflows, and visual inspection all pass. Source: existing completion standard in [`doc/mvp/validation.md`](../../doc/mvp/validation.md).
